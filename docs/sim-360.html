3DoF / 6DoF

The central distinction in VR, and the causes of simulator sickness.

You will not wear a headset in this course. You still have to be able to sit in a meeting and say whether a proposal needs one, which kind, and what it will do to the people who try it. This is that conversation, made visible.

Why people get sick

Motion sickness in VR is a disagreement. Your eyes report motion; your inner ear reports none, or reports it late. The brain resolves that disagreement as poisoning, and does what it does about poisoning.

CauseWhat to do about it
Latency above ~20 msA hardware and engine problem, not a design one. If a vendor will not state their number, that is the answer.
Frame rate below ~72 fpsReduce scene complexity until the frame rate holds. The performance budget takes precedence over visual detail.
Artificial movement, sliding without walkingTeleport instead. Or keep the viewer seated and bring the content to them.
Wide field of view during motionVignetting reduces discomfort at low cost. The view narrows while the viewer moves and opens again when the viewer stops.
Rotation applied by softwareNever turn the camera for someone. Let them turn their own head.
Position tracked but not rotation, or the reverseA degrees-of-freedom mismatch is among the most reliable causes of discomfort. It should be resolved before release.

Where this touches your work

A 360° capture of a store, a show, or an installation is a genuine fashion-communication deliverable and costs almost nothing: a phone and a tripod, or an AI-generated sky in a tool like Blockade Labs. It is 3DoF, and that is fine. Say so in the brief. The moment someone says "and then they walk around the shop", the budget changes by an order of magnitude, and your job in that meeting is to be the person who says it out loud early.
