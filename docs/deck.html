Activate printLookbooks, packaging, swing tags, magazines, out-of-home

Equipment

Equipment used in the course

The course uses no headsets and no programming. Every exercise runs on a mid-range phone, on the hardware the students already carry.

This is the ordinary condition of commercial work rather than a reduced version of it. An experience that requires hardware the audience does not own has no audience.

The continuum,
and six terms.

The terms defined in this section are used for the rest of the course.

The reality-to-virtuality continuum

Augmented reality and virtual reality are positions on one continuum rather than two separate categories. The continuum runs from the entirely real to the entirely virtual. Any given brief occupies a position on it, and that position determines the hardware, the budget and the reachable audience.

Fig. 1. The continuum from the real room to a fully virtual one. The hardware requirement changes between MR and VR.

Move along it yourself: sim-continuum.html

Comparison

Reach and depth compared

Augmented reality

The content is added to a situation the viewer is already in. No decision is required before the content appears, and no equipment is bought for it. Reach is wide and the depth of each encounter is low.

Virtual reality

The viewer chooses to enter and is isolated inside. The encounter is longer and more controlled. Production costs are higher and the audience is smaller by orders of magnitude.

Vocabulary

Six terms used throughout the course

WordPlain meaningIn your work
TriggerWhat starts the experienceThe lookbook page, a face, the floor
AnchorWhat the content is stuck toThe page, the nose bridge, the detected floor
TrackingThe camera re-finding the anchor, 30–60× a secondWhy it does not slide when you move
OcclusionReal things correctly hiding virtual thingsYour hand passing in front of a virtual bag

PayloadThe content that actually appearsThe film, the 3D shoe, the price

Constraints and decisions

Five of the six terms name conditions that the device imposes. Tracking, anchoring, occlusion and drift are properties of the software, and the communicator does not build them.

Two decisions remain with the communicator: the choice of trigger and the design of the payload. Both are design decisions and both are assessed.

Image targets,
and why they fail.

Feature points in an image target

The camera does not compare pictures. It detects feature points: small, high-contrast, asymmetric patches that it can recognise again from a different angle. Corners, edge junctions and the join between two textures are the usual sources.

Fig. 2. Two targets of the same size and the same print cost. The left target yields four feature points. The right target yields more than sixty.

Four properties of a trackable target

Contrast

Local light-to-dark transitions. Not overall brightness.

Even spread

Detail across the whole frame, not clustered in one corner.

Asymmetry

A symmetrical target confuses orientation. Content jumps between rotations.

Uniqueness

No repeats. Every tile of a regular print looks identical to the phone.

One physical property is added to the four: matte stock. A gloss finish under a single ceiling light produces a specular highlight, and no feature points are detected inside it.

Conflicts

Conflicts between design convention and tracking

Your trained instinctWhy it fails as a target
Elegant white spaceNothing to find
Soft gradient backdropNo contrast transitions at all
A clean repeating printNot unique. Every repeat is identical
Minimal type on a flat fieldAll the detail in one small area
Glossy premium stockSpecular highlight wipes the features out

Reconciling the two sets of requirements

The properties that a tracker requires and the properties that the students have been trained to produce are in conflict on most pages. The task is to produce a single page that satisfies both sets of requirements. That reconciliation is the design problem of this session.

Face tracking and
body segmentation.

Including the section on consent and representation, which is assessed.

Face tracking as landmark fitting

Fig. 3. The tracker returns landmark positions rather than pixels. Content attaches to a landmark, and the mesh carries it.

The same principle applies to the body. A segmentation mask separates the person from the background. The mask is accurate enough for colour, print and background replacement. It is not accurate enough to represent the drape of a garment.

Limits of the technique

Look and fit

A phone try-on represents the appearance of a product on a body. It does not represent the fit, because no measurement of the body is taken and no material behaviour is computed.

Represented accurately

Eyewear, earrings, lipstick, hair colour, headwear and nails. Rigid objects attached to stable landmarks.

Simulated, not modelled

Draped garments, fabric weight, the fall of a kurta, the pleating of a saree. These require cloth physics, which a phone filter does not compute.

Stating this limit in a client meeting is part of briefing the work accurately.

Ethics

Four decisions taken by the communicator

Body imageA filter that slims a jaw or lightens skin is a brand position, whether or not you intended one. Defaults are statements.
BiasTrackers are trained on skewed datasets. They fail more on darker skin, on larger bodies, on covered hair. Test across the skin-tone range in your own classroom. If it fails on a classmate, it will fail on a customer.
ConsentFace data is biometric data. Name who stores it, for how long, and how the user agreed.
Cultural weightA bindi, a turban, a saree drape, a dupatta are not decoration to be applied to a stranger's face as an effect. Know what you are putting on someone.

Defaults as design decisions

The tracking model cannot be corrected by the communicator. The defaults applied on top of it can. The starting position of a beauty slider, the state of a skin-lightening effect, and the range of faces used in testing are set by the person who builds the filter.

3D capture
without modelling.

No desktop 3D software is used in this course.

Three methods of 3D capture

MethodHowGood for / weak at
PhotogrammetryMany photos, software solves the shapeAny phone. Needs texture and even light. Hates glass, chrome, black.
LiDARDepth sensor on Pro iPhones / iPadsFast, reliable geometry. Softer texture detail. Not on most phones.
Gaussian splattingA cloud of coloured blobs, not a meshVisually convincing. It does not yet export as an interchangeable .glb, so it is a method to follow rather than to commission.

Free apps that do this on a phone today: Polycam, Scaniverse. The free tiers carry watermarks or export limits. Check them before class.

The technique

The three-orbit capture method

Then turn the object over and capture the underside as a second scan. A surface that the camera never saw cannot be recovered in processing.

Conditions that destroy a scan

Glass, chrome, sequinsThe surface reflects the room, not itself. The software has nothing stable to match
Pure black, pure whiteNo feature points, same as a flat target
Moving the objectMove yourself, not the thing
Hard shadowsShadows bake into the texture and travel with the model forever
Soft, floppy fabricIt changes shape while you circle it
A busy backgroundHalf the room ends up inside your model

The conditions to aim for are an overcast window, a plain matte sheet under the object, and a rigid, textured hero piece: a boot, a bag, a buckle or a bottle.

The numbers

File formats and mobile budgets

.glb

The standard for the web and for Android. One self-contained file. This is the master asset.

.usdz

The format that AR Quick Look on iPhone requires. Export both formats from every scan.

Mobile budget

Triangles≤ 100,000
Texture≤ 2048 px
File size≤ 10 MB

Above these values, phones drop frames or fail to load the file.

Skill drill

Drill: scanning the hero object

  1. Set up: matte sheet, soft even light, object isolated, nothing shiny.
  2. Three orbits: high, middle, low. Feet moving, wrist steady.
  3. Flip it. Second scan for the underside.
  4. Process, crop the ground plane, delete the floating debris.
  5. Export .glb and .usdz. Check both against the budget in tool-asset-check.html.

If the scan fails, substitute a Creative Commons model from Poly Pizza or Sketchfab and credit it. The budget still applies, and the jury asks for the cause of the failure.

Plane tracking
in real rooms.

Plane detection without a marker

  1. The camera picks out feature points on floors, rugs, tables, walls.
  2. The motion sensors track how the phone itself moves through space.
  3. Points that sit on a common flat surface become a plane.
  4. Your object is anchored to that plane, and the phone re-finds the plane every frame.

The method fails on a plain marble floor, a white rug or a dark room. The rule from Day One holds here unchanged: no texture, no tracking.

Watch anchoring, drift and re-acquisition in slow motion: sim-anchors.html

Scale

True scale: one unit is one metre

Consequence

A handbag that appears at the size of a sofa is a scale error in the export. The viewer reads it as an error in the product.

Check

Place the virtual object next to the real one and photograph both in one frame. A mismatch indicates the export scale, not the room.

Scans are often exported in centimetres or inches. Verify each one against the physical piece.

Contact shadow and perceived contact

Fig. 4. The same model under the same lighting, without and with a contact shadow.

Perceived realism depends less on the polygon count than on whether the object appears to touch the floor. A contact shadow of low opacity, tight to the base of the object, produces that reading.

When the third dimension is justified

If nothing in the object changes as the viewer moves around it, the piece does not require augmented reality. A photograph is cheaper, faster and more reliable.

The third dimension is justified by something that only movement reveals: a hidden detail, a sole, a lining, a back panel, or a change in the object as the viewing angle changes.

Virtual reality,
without a headset.

Sufficient to brief a VR project, and to decline one on informed grounds.

Presence and degrees of freedom

Presence

Presence is the state in which the body responds to a virtual environment as though it were a physical one. It is unstable. A dropped frame, an incorrect scale, or a hand that passes through a wall ends it.

Presence is the condition that the other technical decisions support.

A 360° film on a phone is 3DoF. A headset that allows the viewer to step behind a mannequin is 6DoF. The two differ by an order of magnitude in production cost.

Causes and mitigations of simulator sickness

Causes

  • Eyes report motion the inner ear does not
  • Low or unstable frame rate
  • Forced camera movement the viewer did not ask for
  • Wrong scale, wrong horizon

Mitigations

  • Let the viewer move themselves
  • Teleport instead of gliding
  • Keep the horizon fixed and level
  • Short sessions, under five minutes

On your phone now Open sim-360.html and move the handset like a window into a virtual space. Then a Blockade Labs Skybox AI backdrop for your own capsule. This is the extent of the virtual reality work in this course. It is sufficient to brief a vendor and to read a proposal.

Distribution.

Distribution is what distinguishes a project from a campaign.

Installation as a barrier

A campaign that requires an application to be installed loses most of its audience at the installation step. The tendency is consistent across published campaign data.

Web-based augmented reality runs from a link or a QR code in the browser already present on the phone. Its technical ceiling is lower than that of a native application. The number of people who can open it at all is much higher. For communication work that trade usually favours reach over fidelity.

The budget

The eight-second load budget

A 40 MB hero model opened on a 4G connection in a market street does not load inside the budget. The file size is therefore an audience decision and not only a quality one.

The printed page as part of the interface

On the page

  • QR at least 3 cm, with quiet space around it
  • A verb, not a noun: "Point your camera here"
  • Matte stock, always
  • State what will appear. The statement forms part of the payoff

Accessibility

Captions on any audio. Sufficient contrast. A non-AR fallback as well: a plain page for the person whose phone is old, whose data is off, or who does not want to open the camera.

An experience with no alternative route to the content excludes part of its audience.

Briefing a vendor

Five questions for an augmented reality proposal

  1. What is the trigger, and what happens when it fails?
  2. Web or app, and what does that do to reach?
  3. What is the total load size on 4G?
  4. Which devices did you test, and which ones do not work?
  5. Who owns the assets and the analytics when the campaign ends?

The competence this course develops is not the construction of tracking. It is the judgement of whether a proposal is technically plausible and accurately costed.

The work underneath the augmented reality

If nothing remains, the augmented reality was carrying the campaign on its own, and the communication underneath it was thin.

Summary of the course

Six terms

Trigger, anchor, tracking, occlusion, drift and payload. Sufficient to follow and question an augmented reality brief.

One diagnostic rule

No texture, no tracking. The rule accounts for the image target, the scan and the room.

Three completed artifacts

Print, face and room, produced on a personal phone without code or budget.

One test applied to any brief

Whether the piece uses the third dimension, or whether a photograph would serve the same purpose.
