Fundamentals

Definitions, technology stack and current constraints of extended reality.

Scope

Scope of this session

This session defines the terms used in the workshop that follows. The terms come from the literature on the metaverse and on spatial computing. Most of them describe technical conditions rather than products, and they are used here in that sense.

The session covers four areas: the definition of the metaverse and its properties, the four-layer technology stack, the platform record from 2003 onward, and the constraints that remain unresolved. The workshop that follows works within one layer of the stack, on phone hardware.

Section one

Definitions

The term, its properties, and its boundaries

Definition

Definition of the metaverse

The metaverse is the convergence of persistent virtual worlds, augmented reality and the internet into shared three-dimensional space. It is not a single platform and it has no single operator.

The difference from earlier media is in the interface. A website is navigated by clicking between pages. A three-dimensional environment is navigated by movement, and the position of a user within it carries information. Two users adjacent in a space have a spatial relationship that two users listed in a feed do not have.

Properties

Six defining properties

01

Persistence

The environment continues to run after a user leaves. Changes made to it remain in place.

02

Synchronous presence

Multiple users occupy the environment at the same time and observe each other act.

03

Embodied interaction

Each user is represented by an avatar. Distance, orientation and gesture carry information.

04

User-generated content

Users build and modify the environment. The platform supplies the environment, not the contents.

05

Economic activity

Goods are produced, priced and sold, and value is exchanged with currency outside the environment.

06

Identity continuity

The avatar and its possessions persist across sessions, and in principle across platforms.

Most current platforms satisfy the first five properties. The sixth is not satisfied by any of them.

World models

Persistent and session-based worlds

ConditionPersistent worldSession-based world
State on exitStored and carried forwardDiscarded at the end of the session
Form a brand can takeA structure that remains in place and accumulates visitorsAn event that runs for a scheduled period
Operating costServers and moderation running continuouslyCapacity only while the session is live
Design consequenceAccumulation, wear and clutter must be planned forThe opening sequence carries the whole idea

The distinction affects the brief and the budget. A release inside a session-based world is scheduled and costed as an event. A presence inside a persistent world is costed as continuous operation.

Boundaries

Distinctions from adjacent media

Compared withThe difference that matters
A websiteA site is navigated by clicking between pages. An environment is navigated by movement, and the route taken forms part of the communication.
A video gameA game defines an objective and measures progress against it. A world defines no objective, and open-ended activity is the intended use.
Social mediaA feed ranks and delivers content to a user. An environment places users together, where attention is held by co-presence rather than by scrolling.

Section two

Technology
stack

Four layers, and the layer this workshop uses

Architecture

The four-layer stack

Layer 1

3D engine

Unreal, Unity or a proprietary engine. It renders the frame and runs the physics. The platform selects it, not the client.

Layer 2

Networking

It maintains one shared state across many users. Failures at this layer appear as lag, as position corrections, or as an object in two places.

Layer 3

Avatar and identity

The user representation, the possessions attached to it and the garments applied to it. Fashion products are sold into this layer.

Layer 4

Spatial computing

The connection to the physical room: tracking, plane detection, face tracking, occlusion. The workshop operates in this layer.

Delivery

Spatial computing and its delivery modes

Spatial computing is a computer maintaining a model of the space around it. It records the position of surfaces, of the user and of objects placed by the user. The capability is delivered in three modes.

ModeWhat the user seesHardware available here
ARThe physical room, with content registered to itEvery phone in the room
MRThe physical room, with content that responds to its geometryHeadsets with passthrough
VRA replacement environment, with the physical room removedNone. Covered as literacy

The workshop builds in the first mode. The third is covered in theory only.

Open problem

Interoperability

Standards exist for the file format. Standards do not exist for ownership records, for economies or for rendering rules. An asset therefore transfers between platforms as geometry, without the rights and the behaviour attached to it. A garment purchased in one world does not appear in the next.

StandardWhat it definesStatus
glTF / .glbGeometry, materials and animation. The transport format for the webIn use, widely adopted
VRMA humanoid avatar with a defined skeleton and expression setIn use within a narrow band
OpenXRA single interface between an application and headset hardwareAdopted by most headset manufacturers
Metaverse Standards ForumCoordination between the parties who would have to agreeConvening. Not binding

Of these, students use .glb directly. Every model produced in the workshop is exported to that format.

Section three

Platform
record

Two decades of platforms, and the constraints that remain

History

Platform history, 2003 to 2024

PlatformWhat it established
Second Life, 2003That residents produce the contents themselves, and that an internal economy can be converted to national currency
Minecraft, 2011That a modifiable environment retains users for longer than a finished one
Roblox, 2006 onwardThat the creator base can consist largely of children, and that the platform functions as a marketplace
Fortnite, 2017 onwardThat concerts and garment sales can run inside a session-based world at large scale
VRChat, 2014That social presence, rather than rendering quality, determines retention in a headset
Apple Vision Pro, 2024That passthrough mixed reality is manufacturable, at a price that restricts the audience

2021 to 2023

The investment cycle and the technical record

Investment during this period was made on the expectation of a single shared world with a single economy. That expectation was not met and funding was withdrawn. The withdrawal received most of the reporting.

Over the same period, tracking accuracy improved, headset mass decreased, passthrough reached production, and web-delivered augmented reality became reliable on mid-range phones. Investment and technical capability moved independently. Treating one as a measure of the other is a common error in briefing documents.

Constraints

Current technical constraints

  • Comfort. Simulator sickness occurs wherever visual motion is presented that the vestibular system does not report.
  • Identity across platforms. No account carries an avatar and its possessions between worlds.
  • Bandwidth. High-fidelity shared space requires throughput and latency that most connections do not provide.
  • Moderation. Conduct in a live environment is harder to review than a post, and the effect on other users is immediate.
  • Economics. Virtual goods retain value only while the platform retains users.
  • Hardware access. Headsets remain a minority device. Phones do not.

The last constraint determines the format of this workshop. The phone-only requirement follows from the current distribution of hardware, not from the teaching method.

Workshop

Scope of the workshop

Of the material defined in this session, the workshop uses layer four, delivered as augmented reality, in a phone browser, with assets transported as .glb. Persistence, avatar economies and interoperability are covered as reading. They set the terms against which a vendor quotes.

The course deck begins with a printed page, because a printed page can be tracked by a phone camera.
