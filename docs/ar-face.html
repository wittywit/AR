Face → AR

The Brand Filter. Landmarks, not pixels, and the limit of try-on.

A face tracker does not see your face. It fits a mesh of several hundred points to it, every frame, and hands you a set of named positions on that mesh: the forehead, the nose bridge, a cheek, the chin. Everything you can build is a matter of which point you attach to. Turn the mesh on below and you can watch the surface the tracker is actually fitting.

This page runs on the 8th Wall engine, which its owners released under the MIT licence when the hosted platform was withdrawn in February 2026. It needs no account and no build step; the engine and its face model are fetched from a public CDN when you press the button.

The anchors are named, not numbered, and the names are the vocabulary to use when briefing a vendor. The rigid, stable anchors are the ones that work. Anything needing the mesh to deform like fabric is where phone try-on stops being honest.

The evaluative criterion

The test is whether it is opened a second time.

A filter that offers only novelty is opened once. A filter that carries a brand idea is reused, because it lends the wearer a recognisable appearance. A signature colour, a house motif, a season's silhouette. The second is the design target here.

Where this genuinely works, and where it does not

WorksSimulated, not modelled
Eyewear, earrings, lipstick, hair colour, headwear, nails, bindis, brow and lash work. Rigid things sitting on stable landmarks. Draped garments, fabric weight, how a kurta actually falls, how a saree pleats, fit across a real body. That is cloth physics, and no phone filter is doing it.

The distinction is worth stating plainly in a client meeting: phone try-on represents the look, not the fit.

Consent, in one paragraph

You are pointing a camera at a person's face. Tell people what is captured, what is kept, and what is not. Nothing here leaves the device. There is no upload and no server. When you brief a commercial vendor, ask them to state the same thing in writing, and check whether the "beauty" defaults quietly slim, lighten or reshape the face. Publishing that without saying so is a decision, and it is yours.
