Anchors, Drift & Loss

Four of the six words, in slow motion: anchor, tracking, occlusion, drift.

This is a fake camera view. Drag inside it, or let it sway on its own, and watch what the anchor does. Everything that goes wrong in a real AR demo goes wrong here first. Here the cause stays visible.

Tracking

What each control demonstrates

ControlThe principle it demonstrates
Surface textureReduce it to zero. On a plain wall, a white studio sweep or a minimal cover, the anchor is lost: no texture, no tracking.
LightA good target under poor light behaves like a poor target. A large share of reported AR failures are lighting problems rather than software faults.
OcclusionTurn it off and the object passes straight through the pillar. Nothing destroys belief faster, and nothing is harder to get right on a phone.
Contact shadowTurn it off and the object stops standing on the floor and starts floating in front of it. One soft ellipse is the cheapest believability you will ever buy.
Re-anchorWhat a user does instinctively when it drifts. Notice that they have to point back at the trigger. Design for that, and do not hope it never happens.

The three anchor types, with their failure conditions

AnchorHolds becauseFails when
Image targetIt recognises a specific, textured, flat thing you designed.The page leaves the frame, folds, curls, or catches a reflection.
PlaneIt found a flat surface with enough visual detail to lock to.The floor is plain, glossy, dark, or the user is walking.
FaceLandmarks are far more stable than the general world.Extreme angles, hair over the face, more than one face, bad backlight.
