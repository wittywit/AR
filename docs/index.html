Extended Reality

A three-day, phone-only augmented reality workshop for fashion communication.

Every tool listed here runs inside a phone browser. There is no app store, no account and no code. Open this page twice, once on the classroom projector and once on your own phone. The tools are used during the session, not read before it.

Day 1: See

A printed page becomes a trigger. The camera holds some images and loses others, and the difference can be measured before anything goes to print. Day 1 produces the first artifact, The Living Lookbook.

Sequence

  1. The Continuum. Locate the point on the continuum where a headset becomes necessary.
  2. Trackability Scorer. Score the lookbook spread before it goes to print.
  3. Print to AR. Attach a payload to the printed target and hold the phone on it.
  4. Anchors, Drift and Loss. Observe tracking loss and re-acquisition in slow motion.
  5. Storyboard. Fill one four-beat sheet for each of the three artifacts.

Worksheets carry the printed sheets for every activity on this day.

Leave today with

  • A trackability score for your own lookbook spread, and the reason behind it.
  • One printed target that triggers content on a phone.
  • A filled beat sheet for all three artifacts.

Day 2: Wear

The camera turns around and the face becomes the surface. Face tracking reports landmarks and a mesh. It reports nothing about fabric, fit or weight, which is where try-on stops being a measurement. Day 2 produces the second artifact, The Brand Filter.

Sequence

  1. Face to AR. Attach objects to named landmarks on the tracked mesh.
  2. Trackability Scorer. Re-score the spread after the reshoot.
  3. Asset check. Submit the scan of the garment and read the pass or fail.
  4. Place to AR. First placement of the scanned object, at true scale.

Leave today with

  • A working face effect carrying a brand idea, not a novelty.
  • One 3D capture of a real garment or accessory, inside budget.
  • A written statement of what the effect shows that a photograph cannot.

Day 3: Place & Publish

An object lands on the floor plane and holds the size it would have in the room. Day 3 fixes the vocabulary used to brief a vendor, produces The Placed Product, and puts all three artifacts in front of a jury.

Sequence

  1. Anchors, Drift and Loss. Plane anchoring, scale and the contact shadow.
  2. Asset check. The gate. No model is published until it passes.
  3. Place to AR. Final build and the jury demonstration.
  4. 3DoF and 6DoF. Degrees of freedom, and the causes of simulator sickness.

Worksheets carries the jury scoring card.

Leave today with

  • Three linked artifacts under one capsule idea.
  • A one-page spec any developer could quote against.
  • A written statement of what remains of the campaign once the phone is removed.

Reference

The six words

Six words carry most technical discussion of AR. They are introduced on day one and used in the same sense for the rest of the workshop.

WordWhat it meansSaid out loud
TriggerWhat starts the experience."The trigger is the back cover of the lookbook."
AnchorWhat the content is attached to."Anchor it to the floor plane, not the image."
TrackingHow the system keeps up as the camera moves."Tracking drops when they walk past the window."
OcclusionReal things correctly hiding virtual things."Without occlusion the bag floats through the table."
DriftThe anchor slowly sliding out of place."It drifts about a centimetre every thirty seconds."
PayloadWhat gets delivered: the model, the video or the sound."Keep the payload under ten megabytes."

One diagnostic rule

No texture, no tracking.

A blank wall gives the camera nothing to hold. So does a white studio sweep, and so does a cover with one logo in the centre. The same shortage explains the dead image target, the failed room scan and the sliding anchor.

Running these pages

Pages marked camera need HTTPS or localhost. Browsers refuse camera access on any other origin. From this folder:

python3 -m http.server 8000

then open http://localhost:8000/ on the machine itself, or serve the folder over your classroom network and reach it by its address. See README.md for the offline fallback.
