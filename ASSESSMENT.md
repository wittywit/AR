# Brief, rubrics, and crit sheets

---

## 1. THE BRIEF: "XR Capsule"

**Hand out at Day 1, 2:15.**

> You are the communication team for a **6-piece capsule collection.** Use a brand from your portfolio or invent one. It needs a name, a point of view, and a customer you can describe in one sentence.
>
> Over three days you will produce three AR artifacts. Together they must read as **one campaign**, not three unrelated exercises.
>
> **1. The Living Lookbook.** One printed page. Pointing a phone at it reveals the collection. The print must still work as print if nobody scans it.
>
> **2. The Brand Filter.** A face or accessory AR effect carrying your own textile print or brand mark. One interaction, not five.
>
> **3. The Placed Product.** Your hero piece, 3D-scanned on a phone, placed at true scale in a real room.
>
> **Plus:** a 60-second case reel and a one-page campaign sheet.
>
> **Teams of 2–3. Everything shot, scanned, and built on your own phones.**

### Non-negotiables
- One visual system across all three artifacts. Type, colour, and tone must match.
- The printed page must carry a **legible instruction line.** If a stranger cannot work out what to do in 8 seconds, it has failed.
- True scale on the placed product. A bag the size of a sofa is an automatic fail on that artifact.
- Every AR piece must be tested on **a phone that is not yours.**

## 2. GRADING RUBRIC: 100 marks

| # | Criterion | Marks | What full marks looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Campaign coherence** | 20 | The three artifacts obviously belong to one brand. A stranger seeing only one could guess the other two. |
| 2 | **The Living Lookbook** | 15 | Target tracks fast and holds steady. Reveal is motivated by the design, not pasted on. Print stands alone. |
| 3 | **The Brand Filter** | 15 | Anchors cleanly, does not drift on a moving face, carries the brand without a logo slapped on a forehead. One interaction, well chosen. |
| 4 | **The Placed Product** | 15 | Clean scan within budget. True scale. Shadow contact reads. Survives walking around it. |
| 5 | **Craft of the AR itself** | 10 | Loads fast, no drift, no jitter, sensible failure behaviour when tracking is lost. |
| 6 | **Vocabulary & reasoning** | 10 | In the jury, the team explains its choices using *trigger, anchor, tracking, occlusion, drift, payload* correctly. Can defend why AR was the right medium here. |
| 7 | **The reel and the sheet** | 10 | Documents the experience itself, not a screen recording of a menu. |
| 8 | **Process** | 5 | Storyboard, target clinic before/after, asset-check pass, and evidence of testing on other people's phones. |

**Distinction requires full marks on #6.** A student who builds a beautiful filter but cannot say *why the anchor drifted* has learned a template, not a medium.

### Automatic deductions
| | |
|---|---|
| Never tested on another person's phone | −10 |
| Placed product not at true scale | −10 |
| Instruction line missing or illegible on the print piece | −5 |
| A borrowed 3D model passed off as a scan | −15 |

## 7. The one question to ask every team at the jury

> **"If the phone was taken away, what is left?"**

If the answer is "nothing," the AR was decoration. If the answer is "a strong printed campaign that is extended by a phone," the capsule has been understood, which is the objective of the module.
